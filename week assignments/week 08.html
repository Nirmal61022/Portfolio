ProtoSem Journal – Week 08

ROS and Differential Drive Bot Design

This week focused on learning the fundamentals of the Robot Operating System (ROS) and exploring the workflow for designing and visualizing a mobile robot. I attended a ROS session, set up an Ubuntu environment using Oracle VirtualBox, and designed a differential drive robot in Fusion 360. I then exported the model to Ubuntu and used RViz to inspect the robot's dimensions and component positions.

What I learned

  • Understood the basic concepts of Robot Operating System (ROS) and its role in robotics development.
  • Learned how to install and configure Ubuntu using Oracle VirtualBox to create a Linux environment for robotics applications.
  • Designed a differential drive robot in Fusion 360 with two main drive wheels and one caster wheel.
  • Explored the process of exporting a 3D robot model from Fusion 360 to an Ubuntu-based robotics environment.
  • Used RViz to visualize the robot model and examine its dimensions, orientation, and component positions.
  • Gained an initial understanding of the connection between mechanical CAD design and robotic simulation workflows.

Big takeaway

Robotics development involves more than mechanical design alone. Integrating CAD models with a Linux-based robotics environment helps bridge the gap between physical robot design and software-based visualization. Understanding this workflow is an important first step toward robot simulation and autonomous navigation.

Learning reflection

Robot Operating System (ROS)

During the ROS session, I was introduced to the role of ROS in robotics development. I learned about the importance of a suitable software environment and installed Ubuntu through Oracle VirtualBox to prepare for working with robotics tools. This helped me understand how Linux-based environments support robotics development and simulation.

Differential drive bot design

I designed a differential drive robot in Fusion 360, consisting of two main drive wheels and one caster wheel. This design introduced me to the mechanical structure of a mobile robot and the basic configuration used for differential drive motion, where the relative speeds of the two driven wheels control the robot's movement and turning.

CAD integration and RViz visualization

After completing the CAD model, I exported it to Ubuntu and used RViz to inspect its dimensions and component positions. Although the session was interrupted before I could complete the entire workflow, the activity gave me practical exposure to integrating a mechanical model with a robotics visualization environment. It also helped me identify the next steps for improving the model integration process.

Final reflection: Week 8 helped me connect mechanical CAD design with robotics software tools. From configuring Ubuntu and designing a differential drive bot to visualizing the model in RViz, I gained practical exposure to the early stages of a robotics simulation workflow. This experience strengthened my interest in mobile robotics, robot modelling, and software–hardware integration.
